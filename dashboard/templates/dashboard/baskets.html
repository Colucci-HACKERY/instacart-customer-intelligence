Read the relationship, not a promise.

Support

n(A and B) / N, shown as a percentage. How often both products occur in the same basket, out of all nonempty source baskets.

Confidence

n(A and B) / n(A) for A → B. Among baskets containing A, the share that also contain B. Reverse confidence uses n(B) and can differ.

Lift

n(A and B) × N / (n(A) × n(B)). A value above 1 means more co-occurrence than expected under independence; below 1 means less. Lift is symmetric.

Association is not causation. These are observational patterns, not proof that buying one product causes another purchase. Repeated orders from the same customers are not independent trials. Minimum support reduces rare-pair noise; it is not a statistical significance test. Product popularity, promotions and customer preferences may influence the relationships.

Source, processing and scope

Uses original order_products__prior.csv and verified existing product, aisle and department relationships. Each distinct order-product membership contributes once to basket size and pair counts; duplicate source rows still contribute to the row-level reorder denominator. Every unordered pair above the build floor is counted exactly, without sampling or truncating large baskets. Products below the build support threshold cannot form a qualifying pair and are pruned before pair counting.

Two streaming passes keep one basket at a time; sparse counters spill to a temporary disk-backed store. The published snapshot is read on requests, so filtering does not rescan the source file. No product-by-product matrix is created. Empty orders cannot be observed in an order-product file and are excluded. Train/test rows are excluded, and order IDs are used for grouping, not chronology. No prices, revenue or profit are inferred.

Rebuild when source data changes or when a lower pair-support floor is needed. Catalog labels and category filters use the current product catalog; the snapshot's metrics retain their original source population.
