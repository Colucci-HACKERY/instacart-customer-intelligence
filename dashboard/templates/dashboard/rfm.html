Scoring definitions and segment interpretation

RFM Methodology

Recency: measures the relative time since a customer's most recent observed order. Higher recency scores represent more recent purchasing activity.

Frequency: measures the number of orders placed by a customer. Higher frequency indicates stronger purchasing engagement.

Monetary: represents the customer's purchasing value, calculated from accumulated order size.

Each RFM dimension is scored from 1 to 5 using customer percentile positions. The three scores are combined to produce an overall RFM score ranging from 3 to 15.

Strategic Interpretation

Champions: High-value and highly engaged customers. They should be retained through loyalty rewards, personalised offers and exclusive promotions.

Loyal Customers: Customers demonstrating consistent purchasing behaviour. Cross-selling, upselling and loyalty programmes can increase their value.

Potential Loyalists: Customers showing developing engagement. Targeted offers and personalised communication can encourage repeat purchasing.

At Risk: Customers whose purchasing behaviour indicates declining engagement. Win-back campaigns and targeted incentives should be prioritised.

Needs Attention: Lower-engagement customers requiring targeted interventions before significant marketing resources are committed.
